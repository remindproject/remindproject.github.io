Returns to Effort and Growth Mindset

Hello! We are a team of social scientists from Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (UC3M). In REMind we look to explore the role of effort in equality of opportunity, the returns to effort and growth mindset.

Publications Meet the team

Overview

Debates about meritocracy have intensified in recent years. While the economic returns to ability are well documented, the returns to effort remain the missing piece of the puzzle, mainly because effort is hard to measure and to separate from circumstances. REMind aims to empirically assess the impact of effort on socio-economic achievement and to bring growth mindset (the belief that abilities can be developed through effort, learning and persistence) into the study of inequality.

The project combines new and existing data: a three-wave online panel study in Spain of young adults (18–24) and older adults (58–64), a randomized controlled trial of an online growth mindset course, a follow-up of Madrid students from the EFFORT study, and cross-national analyses of PISA data. Effort is measured through a multipronged approach that combines self-reported scales, incentivized real-effort tasks and survey response behavior. The results can inform policies on youth unemployment and social inclusion.

Objectives

🎯

Measure the returns to effort

Estimate the benefits of effort for socio-economic achievement across the life course, and how they differ by social origin, gender and age group in Spain.

🔬

Link growth mindset, effort and achievement

Analyse how growth mindset shapes educational achievement among teenagers through effort, using international PISA data across cultures and genders.

📊

Test a scalable intervention

Evaluate, through an online randomized controlled trial, whether a low-cost growth mindset course can raise effort and later achievement among young adults.
